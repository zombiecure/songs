This song exposes how employment contracts force workers to surrender constitutional rights to get jobs, showing how the First and Seventh Amendments stop at the workplace door while corporations gained unlimited speech rights.

### Supreme Court Workplace Speech Decisions

**Claim: Garcetti v. Ceballos denied workplace speech protection**
- Garcetti v. Ceballos, 547 U.S. 410 (2006)
- 5-4 decision limiting public employee speech rights
- "When public employees speak pursuant to employment duties, no First Amendment protection"
- Private employees have even fewer protections
- Source: Supreme Court opinion and dissents

**The Garcetti Line of Cases**
- Pickering v. Board of Education (1968) - some protection for public concern
- Connick v. Myers (1983) - narrowed public concern definition
- Waters v. Churchill (1994) - employer perception matters
- Borough of Duryea v. Guarnieri (2011) - limited petition clause
- Source: Complete Supreme Court jurisprudence on workplace speech

## The Constitutional Perversion

This song reveals how American workers must surrender constitutional rights as the price of employment. While corporations gained unlimited political speech through Citizens United, workers can be fired for a Facebook post. While corporations enjoy Fourth Amendment protections against searches, workers submit to constant surveillance. While the Seventh Amendment guarantees jury trials, workers must sign that right away or starve. The Constitution has been perverted into a shield for corporate power and a sword against worker rights, creating a system where constitutional freedoms exist only for those who don't need a job to survive.
