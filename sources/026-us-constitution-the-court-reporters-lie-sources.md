#The Court Reporter's Lie - Sources and Documentation

## Summary

This song exposes how court reporter J.C. Bancroft Davis's fraudulent headnote in Santa Clara County v. Southern Pacific Railroad (1886) gave corporations 14th Amendment rights meant for freed slaves, telling the story through farmer Sarah McGinnis who lost her farm to railroad monopoly pricing.

## Key Claims and Sources

### The Santa Clara Case and Court Reporter's Fraud

**Claim: Court reporter J.C. Bancroft Davis added a fraudulent headnote declaring corporations as persons**

- Source: Adam Winkler, "We the Corporations: How American Businesses Won Their Civil Rights" (2018), pp. 117-118
- Source: Thom Hartmann, "Unequal Protection: How Corporations Became People" (2002), Chapter 6
- Source: Howard Jay Graham, "The Conspiracy Theory of the Fourteenth Amendment" (1938) Yale Law Journal
- Source: Supreme Court Records, Santa Clara County v. Southern Pacific Railroad, 118 U.S. 394 (1886)

**Claim: Davis was a former railroad executive with conflicts of interest**

- Source: "Register of the J.C. Bancroft Davis Papers," Library of Congress
- Source: Davis served as president of the Newburgh and New York Railway Company
- Source: Morton J. Horwitz, "Santa Clara Revisited: The Development of Corporate Theory" (1985)

**Claim: The actual Supreme Court opinion never decided corporate personhood**

- Source: The official opinion by Justice Harlan contains no ruling on corporate 14th Amendment rights
- Source: Chief Justice Waite's oral statement was informal and not part of the decision
- Source: Justice Field's concurrence specifically avoided the constitutional question

### Railroad Monopoly Pricing and Farmer Exploitation

**Claim: Railroads charged small farmers double what large shippers paid**

- Source: Frank Norris, "The Octopus" (1901) - based on real Mussel Slough conflict
- Source: Richard White, "Railroaded: The Transcontinentals and the Making of Modern America" (2011)
- Source: California Railroad Commission reports from 1880s documenting discriminatory rates
- Source: Interstate Commerce Commission, "Railways in the United States in 1902" showing pricing disparities

**Claim: Farmers lost their land to railroad monopolies**

- Source: The Mussel Slough Tragedy (1880) - conflict between farmers and Southern Pacific
- Source: "The Farmers' Alliance and Industrial Union" records documenting railroad abuses
- Source: U.S. Senate Committee on Interstate Commerce hearings (1885-1887)

### 14th Amendment Perversion

**Claim: Between 1890-1910, corporations brought 288 14th Amendment cases vs 19 by Black Americans**

- Source: Congressional Record study cited in Connecticut General Life Insurance Co. v. Johnson (1938)
- Source: Justice Hugo Black's dissent in Connecticut General Life Insurance Co. v. Johnson, 303 U.S. 77 (1938)
- Source: Charles Wallace Collins, "The Fourteenth Amendment and the States" (1912)
- Source: Graham, "The 'Conspiracy Theory' of the Fourteenth Amendment" documenting case statistics

**Claim: The 14th Amendment was written for freed slaves, not corporations**

- Source: Congressional Globe, 39th Congress, 1st Session (1866) - debates on 14th Amendment
- Source: Eric Foner, "Reconstruction: America's Unfinished Revolution" (1988)
- Source: Senator Jacob Howard's introduction of the 14th Amendment, May 23, 1866
- Source: Slaughter-House Cases, 83 U.S. 36 (1873) - initial narrow interpretation

### Historical Context

**Claim: Corporate rights established 55 years before many human rights**

- Source: 13th Amendment (1865) - abolishing slavery
- Source: 19th Amendment (1920) - women's suffrage (34 years after Santa Clara)
- Source: Brown v. Board (1954) - meaningful equal protection (68 years after Santa Clara)
- Source: Civil Rights Act (1964) - 78 years after Santa Clara

## Impact and Legacy

This fraudulent headnote became the foundation for all modern corporate constitutional rights, cited in thousands of cases and enabling the corporate domination documented in subsequent songs. The story of Sarah McGinnis represents millions of Americans who lost property and livelihoods to corporate power enabled by this constitutional perversion.
