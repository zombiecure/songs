## About This Song
"Company Scrip" connects the coal mining company store system of the 1920s to modern practices at Amazon and Walmart, showing how America's largest employers use the same exploitation tactics: pay workers so little they need government assistance, then profit from both their labor and their spending.

## The Truth
In Matewan's coal mines, workers were paid in company scrip only spendable at company stores with 10-20% markup. Today, 40% of Amazon warehouse workers need food stamps while Bezos is worth over $170 billion. Walmart employs 1.5 million Americans, many requiring public assistance, while the Walton family holds over $200 billion. The exploitation method remains identical - just the technology changed.

## Why This Matters
This song reveals how working poverty isn't accidental but engineered. When Sheriff J.H. Blair testified in 1921 that "I did all in my power to aid the operators," he was describing a system that continues today through union-busting consultants and algorithms. Understanding this pattern helps workers recognize their common enemy isn't each other but the owner class.
